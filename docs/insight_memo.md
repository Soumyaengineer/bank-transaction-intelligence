# Insight memo

**To:** Priya Menon, Head of Cards
**From:** Soumya Mazumder
**Re:** Where card value sits, and where fraud exposure actually is
**Data:** 13,305,915 transactions, 1,219 active cardholders, Jan 2010 – Oct 2019

---

**Concentrate growth spend on the mass-affluent middle, not the premium tier.**
Customers aged 45–54 earning $30–50K generate the largest share of our $572M in
transaction value — the $30–50K band alone drives $270M (47%), while the $100K+
band contributes $30M (5%).

**Value is moderately concentrated, so retention has leverage.** The top 10% of
cardholders account for 24% of all transaction value. Losing thirty of these
customers costs more than losing three hundred at the bottom.

**Fraud is a channel problem before it is a customer problem.** Of $1.47M in
labelled fraud exposure, $1.06M (72%) came through online transactions, versus
$0.28M chip and $0.13M swipe — despite online being a minority of volume.
Portfolio-wide fraud rate is 0.15%.

**Tightening controls need not hurt our best customers.** The highest-rate
merchant groups — Industrial & Manufacturing (1.09%) and Clothing (0.62%) — are
7× and 4× the portfolio average but carry little of our value; our largest-value
group, Retail & Grocery, runs at 0.18%. Risk and value sit in different places,
so targeted controls beat blanket friction. Two cohorts warrant a closer look:
cardholders 65+ (0.17%, the highest of any age band) and prepaid debit (0.22%).

**Caveat and next step.** Fraud figures cover the 8.9M transactions carrying a
label (67% of total); the remainder is unlabelled and excluded rather than
assumed clean. Next I would size the revenue at risk from online step-up
authentication before recommending it — the control is cheap, the friction is not.
